The Exposure

The threat surface is not one AI system.
It is every system that AI touches.

Enterprise AI doesn't operate in isolation. It integrates with ERP stacks, financial platforms, operational technology, supply chains, and regulated data environments. The governance frameworks protecting those systems were not designed for autonomous agents operating at machine speed.

The result is a new class of systemic risk — one that existing compliance, audit, and security frameworks cannot adequately address. Regulators are beginning to move. The window for proactive positioning is narrow.

01  /  SYSTEMIC CASCADES

AI failure propagates across interconnected systems

An autonomous agent operating outside defined boundaries doesn't produce a contained error. It produces a chain of downstream state changes across every system it touches — often before any human observer can intervene.

02  /  AUDIT BLIND SPOTS

Existing audit trails were not built for autonomous action

Traditional logging captures what happened. It cannot establish that a deterministic, human-authorized decision preceded each consequential AI action. That gap is a material liability under emerging regulatory frameworks.

03  /  REGULATORY EXPOSURE

The compliance window is closing faster than most organizations recognize

EU AI Act Article 14 human oversight requirements are operative. US federal AI governance guidance is accelerating. Organizations without verifiable supervisory architectures face both regulatory and M&A valuation risk.
