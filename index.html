The Case
A New Power Needs a New Code

Whenever mankind has dealt with new power, there has been a stage of imposing governance and control upon it. The time of governing and controlling AI is upon us.

Once humans first broke the muscular power of horses to their will, inventions like the bridle, harness, saddle, and stirrups made the skittish animal controllable, and capable of working without injury.

The power of electricity, at first, was as wild as horses had been: it came with a tremendous risk of electrocution. Over time, safety technologies were developed, and these were standardized in electrical codes required of all new construction, to keep it safe.

While AI brings immense power, it is prone to hallucination and mistakes. These can cause loss of life, damage to property, and financial or operational disasters resulting in immense losses. But we can have an electrical code for AI — one that harnesses its power to work for us while keeping its operation within safe, provable boundaries.

Regulator AI Global owns the electrical code for AI. In a portfolio of 33 provisional patents filed between March 6th and May 19th 2026, we have irrevocably established our priority-of-invention. These are now being stewarded through the process of becoming fully granted patents, while the implementing technology is also in development. This portfolio forms the foundation of deterministic supervisory containment architectures that make safe, auditable AI possible at scale. For a strategic owner, this is the kernel around which a de-facto governance standard for high-risk AI and energy systems is being defined and can be defended.

The Risk

AI doesn't fail in isolation. It fails across interconnected systems — compounding, cascading, and accumulating into losses that no dashboard caught and no insurer will cover.

Aerospace & Orbital

Kessler Syndrome Is One Bad Command Away

Satellite constellations are now too large and too fast for human-only collision avoidance. A single flawed autonomous command — or a miscommunication between agents — could trigger a cascade that renders orbital space unusable for generations.

Finance & M&A

One Hallucination Can Wipe Out Billions

AI is now embedded in program trading, portfolio management, and high-stakes M&A strategy. A hallucinated valuation, a runaway trading loop, or a misread regulatory signal doesn't stay in the model — it moves markets.

Insurance & Liability

Ghost Assets Are the Industry's Nightmare

Hidden, compounding losses that emerge when multiple AI systems interact — unbounded, invisible until they aren't. Insurers are already denying coverage. Re-insurers are pushing back. The window for underwriting this risk is closing.

Healthcare

Missed Diagnoses and Interaction Errors Cost Lives

AI-assisted diagnostics and medication planning carry lethal failure modes. When the system is wrong and no human was watching, there is no audit trail, no accountability, and no way to demonstrate what happened.

Robotics

Good Intentions Don't Prevent Injury

The Three Laws of Robotics are a literary device — not an engineering specification. AI-powered robots in physical environments require provably enforced constraints, not policy documents. The gap between aspiration and enforcement is where people get hurt.

Energy Infrastructure

Orbital Power Transfer Is a Weapon If Misaimed

Wireless power beaming from LEO is approaching commercial reality. The AI managing those beams controls what is, in effect, a directed-energy system. Without deterministic containment, every beam is a liability event waiting to happen.
